# Why Vietnamese refugees built nail salons instead of trade empires

## The question

Why did Vietnamese refugees in the US gravitate toward subsistence businesses (nail salons, pho restaurants, grocery stores) while the Chinese diaspora in SE Asia built scalable trade infrastructure (rice milling, shipping, banking, manufacturing, rubber plantations)? And why did Vietnamese parents develop the "get my kid into a good school so they can escape the life I had" mindset, while Chinese merchant families built businesses worth inheriting across generations?

The answer is not about culture, intelligence, or work ethic. It's about what each group was when they left home, and what businesses were available to them on arrival.

## What the Chinese brought: a merchant class identity

The Chinese who migrated to SE Asia were merchants by occupation. Trade was the reason to leave. They came from coastal trading provinces (Fujian, Guangdong, Hainan) where families had been trading for generations. They arrived with trade skills, existing networks, and often capital.

The level of economic specialization was extraordinary and deeply rooted in dialect-group identity:

- **Hokkien:** Dominated shipping, export/import, hardware merchandising, shipbuilding, banks, rubber plantations. Had "a virtual monopoly of trade at Singapore" in the 1850s. Built on a millennium-old Hokkien trading world providing business connections, trade networks, and resources.
- **Teochew:** Controlled rice trading, enabling them to become the nations' bankers. In Thailand, controlled over 90% of rice mills, pawnshops, and pharmacies. The Red Head Junk merchant fleet was fundamental to South China Sea trade by the late 18th century.
- **Cantonese:** Machinery, manufacturing, groceries.
- **Hakka:** Leather industry, mining, tailoring (nine out of ten tailors in Thailand were Hakka).
- **Hainanese:** Restaurant chains, timber trade, sawmills.

Each dialect group carried specific trade knowledge accumulated over centuries. A Hokkien merchant arriving in Singapore in 1850 was deploying an existing system, not inventing one.

## What the Vietnamese brought: nothing

The ethnic Vietnamese refugees were military officers, civil servants, teachers, farmers, fishermen. South Vietnam's merchant class was largely the Hoa (ethnic Chinese Vietnamese). 70% of boat people in 1978 were ethnically Chinese. The ethnic Vietnamese arrived with no capital, no English, no transferable trade skills, and no diaspora network. The US resettlement program deliberately scattered refugees to prevent concentration.

## The nail salon origin story: an accident of history

In 1975, Tippi Hedren was doing humanitarian work at Hope Village refugee camp near Sacramento. She ran a program for 20 Vietnamese refugee women. The women noticed Hedren's nails. She brought in her personal manicurist, Dusty Coots, to teach them, connected them to Citrus Beauty School for certification, and helped them find salon jobs across Southern California.

The industry became the ideal refugee entry point: low capital, no English required, quick training, network-driven scaling, immediate cash flow. In the 1980s, Charlie Hieu Vo (Vietnamese) and Olivett Robinson (Black American) opened MANTRAP, the first nail salon chain in South LA, pricing services for working-class women.

As of 2015: 51% of US nail technicians are Vietnamese. In California, ~80%. Over 50% of salons nationwide are Vietnamese-owned.

## Subsistence vs. scalable businesses

**Subsistence (Vietnamese pattern):** Low capital entry, low ceiling. Revenue tied to personal labor. Not scalable. No supply chain control. Physically punishing (toluene, formaldehyde, dibutyl phthalate exposure). Not worth inheriting.

**Scalable (Chinese pattern):** Higher entry, unlimited ceiling. Revenue tied to trade volume (leverage). Scalable across borders. Supply chain control creates moats. Worth inheriting and growing.

## The parent psychology: "I do nails so you don't have to"

A Chinese rice merchant in 1850s Bangkok saw a future for his son in the business. Dignified, profitable, scalable. Succession was natural.

A Vietnamese nail salon owner in 1990s California sees chemical fumes, 10-hour days, wrinkled hands. One technician at Advance Beauty College has done nails for 30+ years; her daughter studies sports medicine at Columbia.

The escape mentality is rational given the business type, not a cultural defect. Proof: Vietnamese in tech/finance/medicine don't exhibit it. The pattern is specific to subsistence businesses with physical punishment and low ceilings.

## Implication

The "escape through education" strategy was optimal for 1980. It's suboptimal for 2025. The next generation has access to scalable businesses. The optimal strategy now: build something worth inheriting, not something worth escaping.
