# Four Asian diasporas: 30-year projection (Japanese, Vietnamese, Korean, Chinese)

## The framework

Four variables determine whether a diaspora maintains economic visibility or dissolves:

1. **Homeland gravity** -- does the home country's economy pull people back?
2. **Institutional capital** -- are there community-level economic structures that persist across generations?
3. **Soft power** -- does the homeland produce cultural products that keep younger generations connected?
4. **Intermarriage rate** -- at what pace does the community merge with the host population?

## Japanese: the preview of dissolution

Fourth-generation Yonsei in Hawaii have equal or higher status than white Americans. Intermarriage rate among Yonsei is 34.5% (vs. 9.8% for Nisei). No Japanese economic hub equivalent to Chinatown exists. Japan's stagnation means zero homeland gravity.

**By 2055:** Cultural memory, not economic community. The endpoint of individual integration with no institutional counterweight.

## Vietnamese: on the Japanese trajectory, 50 years behind

Same pattern: education-obsessed parents, professionals who leave, enclave hollowing, rising intermarriage. Two wild cards:

- Vietnam's economic rise could transform diaspora from "refugees who escaped" to "bridge people" (like Chinese post-Deng)
- Deliberate institution building in the next 10-15 years (investment clubs, community-owned real estate, Vietnamese VC networks)

**By 2055:** Without intervention, follows Japanese path. With institutional intervention, could build a Korean-style hybrid.

## Korean: the most interesting case

Three things no other diaspora combines:

1. **Kye (rotating credit associations)** -- closest modern equivalent to Chinese hui/kongsi
2. **State-backed diaspora engagement** -- Korean government actively connects with overseas Koreans (600+ SME deals in 2017 alone)
3. **K-wave soft power** -- K-pop, K-dramas, K-beauty create unprecedented cultural gravity that keeps Gen 3 connected

**By 2055:** Culturally persistent, economically fading. Soft power keeps identity alive, but kye doesn't scale beyond Gen 1, and Koreatowns still hollow out.

## Chinese: persists but faces new pressure

Only diaspora with institutional infrastructure durable enough for generational turnover. But geopolitical toxicity is new: US-China tensions make "Chinese" business identity a liability (CFIUS scrutiny, public suspicion).

**By 2055:** Economic infrastructure persists, may rebrand from "Chinese business network" to "Asian American business network."

## Is the old clan model dead?

Yes, for new diasporas. The conditions (geographic concentration, no banking access, no welfare state, multi-generational proximity) no longer exist.

Potential replacements: digital diaspora networks, ethnic VC funds, alumni association models, church/temple networks that add capital pooling. Nobody has cracked the modern equivalent yet.
