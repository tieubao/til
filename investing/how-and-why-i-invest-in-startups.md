## Context

Notes from a TechCrunch article on startup investing philosophy. The author runs a venture fund focused on people over pure financial models, with their first fund showing 8-9x returns.

## Investment formula

Most VCs optimize for returns with complex economic models. This approach is different: fund the best and brightest people, working on the hardest challenges. Weigh investments on two vectors:

1. **LP returns**: Will the capital have potential for 6-10x returns in 5-10 years?
2. **Happiness**: Are we happy doing the work on behalf of this company?

By measuring both financial return and happiness of being part of the journey, you can holistically gauge the net outcome. Companies that return 5-6x while solving important problems are still good investments.

## Know what you don't know

Mathematical guardrails can box you out of great companies. At the time of investment, it is hard to anticipate which future products become the largest revenue drivers. A mentor's exercise: rank 10 companies by value, then by revenue. The company with the least revenue was the most valuable. The one with the most revenue was the least valuable.

If you only operate on a purely mathematical model, you might not have invested in Facebook.

## What to look for in founders

Plan on working with a founder for 5-10 years. Four factors must all check out:

1. **Domain expertise**: Unique insight that gives them an edge in the problem space.
2. **Grit**: Capacity to persevere through tough situations. No company goes as planned. Assessed mostly by gut instinct.
3. **Purpose**: What they are building connects to a greater purpose they are personally invested in. Belief systems do not go away when challenges come.
4. **Charisma**: When meeting a founder with true charisma, you should feel like quitting your job to work for them. If you do not feel that, the best candidates they try to recruit will not feel it either.

## Red flags in founders

1. **Questionable principles**: Gender equality, racial equality, working with good humans matter. Work with people who represent their brand the way you would represent yours.
2. **Lack of domain expertise**: If they do not know their numbers, it is an immediate killer. If they do not understand the economics and motivational drivers within their sector, they will not build something special.
3. **Lack of respect for time**: Smart people know the right time and way to connect. If they have not considered your time, they probably will not consider others' time either.

## The investor's role beyond cash

The job of the investor goes beyond fueling the company with cash. It is about fueling the company with expertise, intelligence, and connectivity.

Key transitions where investors add value:
- Bootstrap to scale (hiring discipline, avoiding clones of the founder)
- Micromanaging to macromanaging (building divisions)
- Growth-stage fundraising
- Going public or acquisition

Build an investment team with variable experience across different firms and individuals. Do not only target big firms; target the person who understands your needs.

## Core thesis

Find good people solving tough problems, and the financials often sort themselves out.
