# How to Sit at the Table - The Thesis

![Opportunity lens vs money lens](https://assets.han-ws.workers.dev/i/2026/04/wealth-01-opportunity-lens-vs-money-lens.svg)

## Core thesis

Younger people should evaluate relationships with older/wealthier people through the lens of **opportunity access and judgment transfer**, not direct financial gain.

The real value of spending time with people 20+ years older and significantly wealthier is not the possibility of them giving you money. It is what you absorb through conversation and proximity: their pattern recognition, their network effects, and access to the deal flow and opportunities they naturally attract.

## Why the money lens fails

- **Direct transfer** (gift, loan, handout) is a one-time event with no leverage
- Creates **dependency** -- you need them again
- Is **transactional** -- depletes their patience
- Positions you as someone seeking favors, not building alongside them

## Why the opportunity lens works

- **Deal flow proximity**: you see what they see before it becomes public
- **Pattern recognition transfer**: absorb 20 years of judgment through exposure
- **Network access**: their introductions are endorsements of your character
- **Compounds over time**: each insight builds on the last

## The critical nuance

Opportunities without judgment are dangerous. Plenty of younger people get access to deal flow through older contacts and make terrible decisions because they saw the opportunity but didn't have the pattern recognition to evaluate it.

**The highest-value transfer is not the deals they mention. It is the reasoning they use to decide which deals to ignore.**

## What you owe in return

The exchange is not free. You must:
- Be worth their time
- Bring signal (a market insight, a technology trend, a connection), not just presence
- Treat every interaction as a deposit into a multi-year account

## Connected frameworks

- Arab majlis tradition: younger members sit with elders to absorb judgment through exposure, not instruction
- Rothschild mandatory sharing: every member contributes intelligence, not just receives it
- Omi merchants: contribute before extracting, build trust through consistent generosity
- The economics of mentorship: a mentor can transfer in two years what took them ten to learn
