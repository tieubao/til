# Anti-Patterns: Behaviors That Destroy Trust Permanently

The behaviors that destroy trust are more useful to catalog than the behaviors that build it. You can survive making some positive moves poorly. You cannot survive the anti-patterns.

## Personal / dinner context

### 1. The pitch at dinner
You walk into a first meeting and spend the entire time talking about what you want. The other person correctly concludes the relationship will be transactional. It took three years to get another meeting in the origin story from V3.

**Recovery**: possible but slow. Requires 3+ interactions of pure giving with zero asks.

### 2. The one-way consumer
Absorbing without contributing. Listening without bringing signal. You become tolerated, not invested in. Pleasant and forgettable is the most common failure mode.

**Recovery**: possible. Start bringing specific, useful information to every interaction.

### 3. The performance artist
Passes surface tests through charisma and polish. Under real pressure, folds. Tells every person what that person wants to hear. Optimizes for being liked, not reliable. Older people who've been burned by this pattern are specifically looking for the opposite: the person who says "I don't know" honestly.

**Recovery**: extremely difficult. Once they've categorized you as surface, the label sticks.

### 4. The gossip
Talks about others at the table, including competitors. Reveals that your discretion has a limit. They will never share sensitive deal flow with you.

**Recovery**: permanent exclusion from sensitive information. The Omi merchants' rule: the person who speaks badly about one person will eventually speak badly about all.

### 5. The entitlement drift
Starts expecting deal flow as owed rather than earned. The Omi merchant warning: wealth leads to arrogance, arrogance to impoliteness, impoliteness to being hated, and from there straight down.

**Recovery**: possible if caught early. Requires visible return to the deposit-phase behaviors.

### 6. The information leaker
Fails the confidentiality test. Shared info appears in other conversations. This is permanent. Nobody tells you the door closed.

**Recovery**: none. This is a one-shot test.

## Enterprise context

### 1. Optimizing for the invoice
Change-requesting every out-of-scope discovery. Treating the relationship as a series of transactions. You get work but never the pre-RFP call.

### 2. Telling them what they want to hear
Agreeing with unrealistic timelines. Saying "yes" when "that's risky, here's a better path" is the honest answer. The polished-vendor failure mode.

### 3. Only talking to your buyer
Single-threaded relationship. When that person changes roles, you get replaced. Partners have relationships across the organization.

### 4. Disappearing between engagements
Silent between contracts. Partners share intelligence between deals. The relationship persists independent of the transaction.

### 5. Surprising them publicly
Raising concerns in group meetings that should have been flagged privately. Never surprise the director in front of their boss.

## Recovery framework

| Anti-pattern | Recoverable? | Timeline | Method |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pitch at dinner | Yes | 3+ interactions | Pure giving, zero asks |
| One-way consumer | Yes | Immediate | Start bringing signal |
| Performance artist | Rarely | 6+ months | Demonstrate substance under real pressure |
| Gossip | No | Permanent | Cannot un-ring the bell |
| Entitlement drift | Yes, if early | 2-3 months | Return to deposit-phase behavior |
| Information leak | No | Permanent | Door closes silently |
| Invoice optimizer | Yes | 1-2 deals | Absorb costs, share insights freely |
| Yes-man vendor | Rarely | 3+ engagements | Push back on something real, with data |
| Single-threaded | Yes | 1 engagement | Build relationships across their org |
| Ghost between deals | Yes | 3-6 months | Start sharing intel between contracts |
| Public surprise | Yes, once | Immediate | Apologize privately, never repeat |
