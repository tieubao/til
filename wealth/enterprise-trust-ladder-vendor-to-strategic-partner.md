# Enterprise Trust Ladder: From Vendor to Strategic Partner

![Enterprise trust ladder](https://assets.han-ws.workers.dev/i/2026/04/wealth-04-enterprise-trust-ladder.svg)

The same character/value/role-ambiguity dynamic from dinner tables applies to enterprise relationships. Directors run the same invisible tests.

## The 5 rungs

### Rung 1: Cold contact
Make yourself useful before asking. Share a relevant insight. Goal: name recognition, not a deal.

### Rung 2: Pilot deal (THE MITSUI GATE)
Most important moment. Meet terms exactly. One failure resets to zero. They watch everything, not just the deliverable.

### Rung 3: Repeat engagement
Character tests running. Do you deliver bad news proactively? Treat their junior team well? Talk about competitors?

### Rung 4: Trusted vendor (THE PLATEAU)
Reliable but interchangeable. Where most companies get stuck permanently.

### Rung 5: Strategic partner
They consult you before deciding. You see real problems, not scope documents.

## Pilot deal response protocol

Delivery is 40%. Process is 60%.

1. **Over-deliver on scope, under-deliver on drama**: 10-15% more, quietly. Don't invoice for extra.
2. **Communicate like a partner**: Proactive updates before being asked. Share intel outside scope.
3. **Surface problems early**: "This timeline is at risk, here is why, here is what I recommend."
4. **Deliver the insight, not just the output**: Noticed a scaling problem? Tell them.
5. **Nemawashi debrief**: "What worked, what didn't, what would you want different?"

## Enterprise anti-patterns (vendor forever)

- **Optimizing for the invoice**: change-requesting every out-of-scope discovery
- **Telling them what they want to hear**: agreeing with unrealistic timelines
- **Only talking to your buyer**: single-threaded relationship dies when that person moves
- **Disappearing between engagements**: partners share intelligence between deals
- **Surprising them publicly**: never surprise the director in front of their boss
