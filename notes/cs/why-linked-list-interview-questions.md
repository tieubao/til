## Context

A brief historical explanation of why linked list problems became a staple of software engineering interviews, despite their limited relevance to most modern work.

## The common justifications

When asked why linked list questions are used in interviews, people give two answers:

1. "It tests CS fundamentals."
2. "It tests reasoning through a new problem."

## The actual history

In the early 1980s, C programmers were in high demand. Interviewers used questions that specifically tested experience with C, which meant problems involving heavy pointer manipulation. Linked lists were a natural fit for this.

This ingrained linked list questions as a cultural institution at places doing lots of low-level work, especially Microsoft and Google. From there, the practice was exported to the wider software world. Without the original context, people assumed it was about "testing CS fundamentals" or "quick thinking" rather than what it actually was: a C-specific skill test.

## The takeaway

Many interview practices are historical accidents that outlived their original purpose. The linked list question is not a timeless test of engineering ability; it is a cultural artifact of 1980s C programming that became self-perpetuating.
