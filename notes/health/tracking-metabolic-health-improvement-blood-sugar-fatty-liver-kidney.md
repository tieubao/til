## Question

When dieting and exercising to improve fatty liver, blood sugar, and kidney health, can subjective signals (energy, sleep, weight loss) tell you that health is actually improving, or are blood tests required?

## Answer

Subjective signals are not enough. Blood tests are the only reliable way to know.

The reason is that fatty liver and kidney issues are notoriously asymptomatic until they're advanced. You can feel "great" while NAFLD progresses or eGFR drops. This is exactly why these are called silent diseases.

| What you can feel | What stays silent |
|---|---|
| Energy, sleep, mood, gym performance | Liver enzymes (ALT, AST) |
| Clothes fitting, scale weight | Fasting glucose, HbA1c |
| Digestion, less brain fog | Kidney filtration (eGFR, creatinine) |
| Cravings reducing | Triglycerides, LDL |

### The blood test panel that actually matters

Three organ systems, nine markers. Get all of them at baseline so future tests have a comparison point.

![Blood test panel for blood sugar, liver, and kidney](https://assets.han-ws.workers.dev/i/2026/05/health-marker-panel-blood-sugar-liver-kidney.svg)

**Blood sugar (insulin sensitivity):**
- HbA1c: 3-month average glucose. Target under 5.7%. The single best number.
- Fasting glucose: snapshot reading. Target 70-99 mg/dL. Has day-to-day variability.
- Fasting insulin: often skipped by basic panels. Target under 10 uIU/mL. Catches insulin resistance years before glucose goes up.

**Liver (fatty liver):**
- ALT: liver cell damage marker. Target under 30 U/L. Most sensitive to fatty liver changes.
- AST: pairs with ALT. Target under 35 U/L. AST/ALT ratio matters.
- GGT: liver stress signal. Target under 40 U/L. Drops fast when diet is working.

**Kidney:**
- eGFR: filtration rate. Target above 90. The headline number.
- Creatinine: waste product. Target 0.7 to 1.2 mg/dL. Affected by muscle mass.
- Urine ACR (albumin-creatinine ratio): protein leak test. Target under 30. Earliest kidney warning.

Add a lipid panel (triglycerides, HDL, LDL) since lipids are tightly tied to fatty liver progression.

### Realistic timeline before retesting

Different markers respond at different speeds. Don't retest too early or you'll see noise instead of signal.

![Marker improvement timeline](https://assets.han-ws.workers.dev/i/2026/05/marker-improvement-timeline-weeks.svg)

- Triglycerides: 2-4 weeks (fastest mover)
- ALT / GGT: 4-8 weeks
- Fasting glucose: 6-12 weeks
- HbA1c: 10-14 weeks (it's a 3-month average, no point retesting earlier)
- eGFR / kidney function: 12-24 weeks

**Practical retesting cadence:** baseline now, full retest at 12 weeks. Liver enzymes and triglycerides can be spot-checked at 6 weeks if you want early signal.

### Common tracking mistakes

**"I lost 3 kg" is not the same as "my health improved."** Weight loss correlates with health improvement, it doesn't equal it. You can lose 3 kg of muscle and water and barely move metabolic markers. You can also lose 3 kg of visceral fat and dramatically improve every marker. The scale doesn't know which one happened.

**Tracking without a baseline is flying blind.** If you don't have blood test numbers from before the diet, you can only measure improvement against future tests, not against where you started. Get the baseline panel done early.

**Subjective signals can mislead in both directions.** Some people feel terrible during the early phase of fixing fatty liver (electrolyte shifts, keto flu) while their markers are improving fast. Others feel amazing on a crash diet while liver enzymes spike from rapid weight loss.

**Strength as a proxy for metabolic health:** strength going up while losing weight = preserving muscle = metabolic health is genuinely improving. Strength dropping while losing weight = likely losing muscle, which is bad even if the scale moves.

## Key takeaway

Get a baseline panel (HbA1c, fasting glucose, fasting insulin, ALT, AST, GGT, lipid panel, creatinine + eGFR, urine ACR) before starting any serious metabolic intervention. Retest at 12 weeks. The scale and how you feel are not substitutes for these numbers, especially for silent diseases like fatty liver and kidney dysfunction.
