## Context

For tracking metabolic health (blood sugar, fatty liver, kidney) and weight loss, most of the important markers cannot be measured at home. The ones that can be have caveats. This note maps which markers are home-measurable, which need a lab, and what devices are actually worth buying for someone in Vietnam.

## Capability matrix: home vs lab

![Home vs lab capability matrix](https://assets.han-ws.workers.dev/i/2026/05/home-vs-lab-marker-matrix.svg)

**Fully home-measurable:** blood pressure, weight + body composition, heart rate / HRV, waist circumference.

**Partially home-measurable:** blood glucose (finger-prick gives single snapshots, no HbA1c equivalent), CGM sensors (14 days of continuous data, expensive), urine dipsticks (crude protein/ketone screening).

**Lab-only (no home device exists):** ALT/AST/GGT (liver enzymes), eGFR/creatinine (kidney filtration), HbA1c (3-month glucose average), lipid panel.

## Tier 1: Must-haves (under 3M VND total)

### Tape measure (50k VND)
Sounds dumb. Isn't. Waist circumference at the navel is the single best home proxy for visceral fat, which drives fatty liver. Measure weekly, same time, morning, after toilet. Target: under 90 cm for men, under 80 cm for women. This single number tells you more about whether your liver is healing than anything else you can buy.

### Smart body composition scale (500k - 1.5M VND)
**Pick: Xiaomi Mi Body Composition Scale 2** at ~700k VND on Shopee Vietnam.

Honest caveat: home BIA scale readings are not 100% accurate. Don't trust the absolute body fat % number. Trust the **trend**. If it says you went from 28% to 25% over 12 weeks, the trend is real even if the actual numbers are off by 2-3%.

Upgrade options if budget allows: Tanita BC-587 or Omron HBF-375 (2-4M VND) are more accurate. The InBody H20N home version exists but costs 8M+ VND and isn't worth it for a personal user.

### Blood pressure monitor (1M - 2M VND)
Critical and often overlooked. Hypertension travels with fatty liver, insulin resistance, and kidney problems. You need to know your numbers.

**Pick: Omron HEM-7156T** (~1.4M VND on Shopee Mall). Upper arm cuff, Bluetooth to phone, stores 60 readings. Skip wrist models, they're less accurate.

Omron's basic models start around 600k VND. Higher-end models with Bluetooth and memory storage cost up to 3M VND. Omron uses Intellisense technology that auto-adjusts cuff inflation, improving comfort and accuracy.

## Tier 2: Nice to have for these goals

### Blood glucose meter (400k - 800k VND, plus ~6k VND per strip)
Useful only if you actually plan to use it. The trap: people buy one, test 3 times, then abandon it.

**Pick: Accu-Chek Instant or OneTouch Verio** for best balance of accuracy and strip availability in Vietnam.

What's actually useful to test:
- Fasting glucose first thing in the morning (target under 100 mg/dL or 5.6 mmol/L)
- 2 hours after a problem meal (target under 140 mg/dL or 7.8 mmol/L) to identify spike triggers

What's NOT useful: random testing 5 times a day. You'll just stress yourself out without insight.

### Smartwatch with HRV (3M - 12M VND)
Garmin (Forerunner 55 ~4M, Venu Sq ~6M) or Apple Watch tracks resting heart rate and HRV. As metabolic health improves, resting HR drops and HRV rises. These are real, research-backed signals.

If you already own one, use it. If not, don't buy one just for this. The body comp scale + waist measurement give you 80% of the same insight.

## Tier 3: Probably skip

### Continuous Glucose Monitor (CGM)
The shiny option. Abbott FreeStyle Libre is available in Vietnam at ~1.5-2M VND per 14-day sensor. Sensors last 14 days and send glucose readings to a smartphone via Bluetooth.

**Honest take:** for someone without diabetes, a CGM is overkill but educational. Wearing one 14-day sensor (~2M VND) during active dieting will be eye-opening: you'll see exactly which foods spike you and which don't. After that, you don't need to keep wearing them. **One-time use as a learning tool, not a permanent setup.**

### At-home liver/kidney test kits
Things like LetsGetChecked or Everlywell exist in the US but are barely available in Vietnam, and you still mail blood samples to a lab. Skip them. Just go to a real lab in Da Nang.

## Tracking traps to avoid

**Daily weighing causes anxiety.** Weight fluctuates 1-2 kg daily from water, food, hormones, sleep. Weigh once or twice a week, same conditions. The 4-week trend matters, not the daily number.

**Cheap pulse oximeters and "smart rings" don't track liver or kidneys.** Don't get sold on devices that claim "comprehensive health monitoring" when none of their sensors measure what you actually need.

**The Quantified Self trap.** More data does not equal better decisions. If you're not changing behavior based on the numbers, the device is just a toy. Pick 2-3 metrics, track them consistently, ignore the rest.

## Verdict: minimal effective stack for ~2.5M VND

For Vietnam-based metabolic health tracking on a budget:

1. Omron HEM-7156T blood pressure monitor (~1.4M VND)
2. Xiaomi Mi Body Composition Scale 2 (~700k VND)
3. Tape measure (~50k VND)
4. Lab blood test every 12 weeks (~800k-1.5M VND per round at private clinics)

That covers 90% of what you need to know. Everything else is optional or marginal.

If you want one premium add-on: a single FreeStyle Libre 14-day sensor during your most active dieting phase, used as a learning tool to identify food spikes, then discontinued.
