## Context

Companion piece to Hajime Hotta's HR evaluation framework. While [[hr-evaluation-unique-value]] focuses on how organizations should measure people, this note distills the individual's perspective: how to pursue professional excellence by building unique value rather than chasing generic skill development.

## The excellence paradox

Excellence is not about being the best at a common skill. The paradox: the harder you try to be "excellent" at something everyone else is also trying to be excellent at, the less differentiated you become. True excellence comes from finding your unique position.

## Building unique value as an individual

From Hotta's framework, the path to excellence follows a deliberate strategy:

**1. Find the intersection, not the center.** The most valuable professionals sit at the intersection of multiple domains. An engineer who also understands venture investing. A designer who understands behavioral economics. The rarer the combination, the higher the value.

**2. Depth without influence is a dead end.** Going deep into a specialty only matters if you can translate that depth into changed decisions around you. Excellence requires both mastery and communication. The quiet expert who never persuades anyone is undervalued not because the system is broken, but because unexpressed expertise genuinely has less impact.

**3. Reject commoditized paths.** High-demand fields attract high supply. If your growth plan looks identical to a thousand other people's growth plans, you are optimizing for replaceability. Pursue the path that fewer people are taking.

**4. Measure yourself by irreplaceability.** The ultimate question is not "how good am I at X?" but "how hard would it be to find someone else who does what I do?" If the answer is "not very hard," you have a positioning problem, not a skills problem.

## The role of influence

Excellence without influence is invisible. Hotta's formula (Unique Value = Differentiation x Influence) means that an excellent specialist who cannot change how others think or decide has effectively multiplied their differentiation by zero.

Building influence is not about self-promotion. It is about developing the ability to make others see what you see. That is the difference between being knowledgeable and being valuable.
