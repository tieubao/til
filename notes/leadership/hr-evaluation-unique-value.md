## Context

Hajime Hotta's framework for thinking about HR evaluation through the lens of market value. This reframes performance review from checking boxes to measuring genuine professional worth.

## The Problem

Most HR evaluation systems rely on standardized checklists that fail to capture what actually makes someone valuable. They measure commoditized skills rather than what makes a person irreplaceable.

## Core framework: market value

Professional market value follows supply and demand, but the dominant factor is **uniqueness/irreplaceability**. Hotta illustrates this with a simple example: a pen contributes more to intelligence than a $20k car, yet its market value is low because pens are easily substitutable.

**Unique Value = Differentiation x Level of Influence**

Two strategies exist for increasing market value:

1. **Target high-demand fields** - competitive, harder to stand out, commoditization risk
2. **Develop a unique, specialized position** - more achievable, higher long-term returns

Example: AI engineers in high supply command lower premiums than rare AI-focused venture investors, despite having equal or greater technical capability. The investor's unique position at the intersection of domains creates scarcity.

## Influence as the multiplier

Hotta defines influence as "the change of decision of someone." This is the critical missing piece in most evaluation frameworks. Specialized knowledge without the ability to persuade others to adopt your perspective is, in his words, "USELESS."

You can be the most knowledgeable person in a niche, but if that knowledge never changes how others think or act, its organizational value is zero. Influence turns private expertise into shared capability.

## Practical takeaway

Organizations should evaluate people on their unique contributions and influence, not stereotyped technical checklists. Growth planning should focus on:

- Identifying where someone can develop genuine differentiation
- Building influence and persuasion capabilities alongside technical depth
- Respecting specialties that fall outside standard role definitions

The most valuable people are not the ones who score highest on generic rubrics. They are the ones who would be hardest to replace.
