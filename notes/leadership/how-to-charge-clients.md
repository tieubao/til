## Context

Paul Boag at Smashing Magazine shares an honest, practical approach to pricing for freelancers and agencies. The method is refreshingly simple compared to complex value-based pricing theories.

**Source:** [How To Work Out What To Charge Clients: The Honest Version](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2017/08/what-to-charge-clients/)

**Attachment:** [How To Work Out What To Charge Clients - Smashing Magazine.pdf](https://github.com/tieubao/til/files/1680059/How.To.Work.Out.What.To.Charge.Clients.The.Honest.Version.Smashing.Magazine.pdf)

## Calculate your minimum rate first

1. Establish your minimum salary needs
2. Add all costs: software, rent, equipment, one-time purchases
3. Include savings and pension contributions
4. Account for taxes
5. Calculate working days: subtract weekends (260 working days), holidays (~10), vacation (~20)
6. Apply realistic billability: assume only 60% of your time is chargeable

This gives you a floor price that ensures viability.

## Three markup factors

**Interest level:** Projects that sound awesome get barely marked up. Projects that look dull get a hefty wedge added on top. This naturally balances your portfolio.

**Client difficulty:** Assess during initial conversations whether clients will be demanding. Difficult clients warrant premium pricing for the extra effort required.

**Market position:** Large organizations can pay more than smaller ones. Higher fees also signal greater expertise to clients.

## Key mistakes to avoid

- Never charge less when desperate for work
- Avoid basing rates on how busy you are
- Don't treat pricing as purely mathematical

The fundamental principle: "Pricing is about supply and demand. If people are willing to pay, you can charge whatever you like."
