## The coordination math

Hierarchy emerges naturally in self-organizing systems to reduce coordination costs. With 40 engineers in 10 small teams, you need n(n-1)/2 links, meaning 780 interactions just to keep consistency. Fewer, larger teams with managers dramatically reduce this burden.

## Calendar incompatibility

Engineering requires sustained focus. Management demands constant interruptions. These two modes cannot coexist effectively in one person. Separating these roles protects engineers' deep work time.

## Accountability structure

Managers embody responsibility for team health, organizational development, and people growth. Without a dedicated manager, these outcomes will never be anyone's number one job, so they slip.

## The productivity myth

"Your customers give zero fucks how many lines of code you write." True velocity comes from building the right things, not maximizing code output. This requires the strategic coordination that managers provide.

## The real problem

When a CEO prevents a VP Engineering from hiring managers, the CEO is preventing the VP from doing their actual job. That organizational dysfunction "does not bode well" for the company's future.
