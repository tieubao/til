## Context

The Software Engineering Code of Ethics and Professional Practice (Version 5.2), jointly approved by the ACM and IEEE-CS. A foundational document that defines the ethical responsibilities of software engineers across eight principles.

**Source:** [Software Engineering Code of Ethics](http://ethics.acm.org/code-of-ethics/software-engineering-code/)

## The eight principles

Software engineers shall commit themselves to making software development a beneficial and respected profession. The eight principles:

1. **PUBLIC** - Act consistently with the public interest. Accept full responsibility for your own work. Approve software only if you have a well-founded belief it is safe and meets specifications.

2. **CLIENT AND EMPLOYER** - Act in the best interests of client and employer, consistent with public interest. Keep confidential information private. Report promptly if a project is likely to fail or violate IP law.

3. **PRODUCT** - Ensure products meet the highest professional standards. Strive for high quality, acceptable cost, and reasonable schedule. Ensure realistic quantitative estimates with uncertainty assessments.

4. **JUDGMENT** - Maintain integrity and independence in professional judgment. Do not engage in deceptive financial practices. Disclose conflicts of interest.

5. **MANAGEMENT** - Promote an ethical approach to management. Ensure engineers are informed of standards before being held to them. Offer fair remuneration. Do not punish anyone for expressing ethical concerns.

6. **PROFESSION** - Advance the integrity and reputation of the profession. Be accurate in stating software characteristics. Take responsibility for detecting and reporting errors.

7. **COLLEAGUES** - Be fair and supportive. Credit others' work. Review work objectively. Assist colleagues in professional development.

8. **SELF** - Participate in lifelong learning. Improve ability to create safe, reliable, and useful software. Do not give unfair treatment based on irrelevant prejudices.

## Core tension

The code acknowledges that ethical tensions exist and cannot be resolved by blind reliance on rules. Software engineers should consider: who is affected by their work, whether they are treating people with respect, how the public would view their decisions, and how the least empowered will be affected.
