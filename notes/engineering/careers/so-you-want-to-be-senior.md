## Context

An article from CSS-Tricks on the qualities that distinguish senior developers, arguing that seniority is not about coding speed or technical knowledge alone.

**Source:** [So You Want To Be a Senior Developer?](https://css-tricks.com/want-senior-developer/)

## Five qualities of a senior developer

**Experience.** There is no shortcut. You will not roll into your first job as a senior developer, and even a proven senior developer probably should not start as one at a new company. Even if the tech is the same, the people are not.

**A track record of good judgment.** Development is not only about writing code; it is about making choices. Good choices are researched, discussed, and influenced by instinct and experience. Demonstrate that your judgment is informed, ethical, and transparent, over and over.

**Positive impact beyond the code.** If the only thing you contribute is coding chops, you probably are not a strong senior candidate. Soft skills matter: clear emails, roping people into ideas, leading meetings, resolving team friction before it drains productivity.

**Helpful, not all-knowing.** Not knowing the answer does not disqualify you. What matters is how you respond: help find the answer together, do research and follow up. Even when you know the exact answer, digging into the question with your coworker is better than just delivering it and spinning your chair back around.

**A force multiplier.** This is the most important quality. The best senior developer on a team is not the one with the most commits; it is the one who multiplies the effectiveness of everyone else. Creating a solid dev environment, teaching others, freeing up others to be productive. A developer who is a force multiplier for the entire team is absolutely a senior developer.
