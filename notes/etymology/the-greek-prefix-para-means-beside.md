## Definition

The prefix **para-** comes from Ancient Greek **παρά (pará)** and literally means **"beside," "alongside," or "next to."** From that core spatial sense, it stretched into related meanings: "near," "by," "past," "beyond," and sometimes "contrary to" or "against." All later senses trace back to one thing being positioned in relation to another.

## Origin

Greek **παρά** was a preposition of spatial relation. When attached as a prefix to other Greek roots, it placed the new word "alongside" the base concept. The English `para-` we use today is a direct loan of that prefix through Latin and into scientific/scholarly English.

## Examples

The "beside what?" question unlocks most of these:

| Word | Construction | Literal meaning |
|------|--------------|-----------------|
| **paragraph** | para + graphē (writing) | "written beside"; originally a margin stroke, not the text block |
| **parallel** | para + allēlois (one another) | "beside one another" |
| **parasite** | para + sitos (food/grain) | "beside the food"; one who eats at another's table |
| **paramedic** | para + medic | beside/auxiliary to a medic, not the main one |
| **paranormal** | para + normal | beside/beyond the normal |
| **paradox** | para + doxa (opinion) | beside/contrary to opinion |
| **paraphrase** | para + phrasis (speech) | a rewording set beside the original |

**The paragraph case is worth its own note.** In ancient Greek manuscripts, a *paragraphos* (παράγραφος) was a short horizontal stroke drawn in the margin **beside** the text to mark a break, a change of speaker, or a new section. The word originally referred to the **mark itself**, not the block of text. Over centuries, the meaning shifted from "the mark beside the text" to "the section of text the mark indicated." So *paragraph* literally means "a thing written beside."

## Mental model

When you see `para-`, ask: **"beside what?"** The answer almost always unlocks the word.

- Paramedic? Beside a doctor.
- Paranormal? Beside ordinary reality.
- Paragraph? Beside the text.
- Parasite? Beside the food.

## Footnote on "graph"

`graph` on its own does not etymologically mean "diagram." It comes from Greek **graphē** meaning "writing" or "drawing," from the verb *graphein* (to write, to scratch, to draw). The "chart/diagram" sense is a much later English specialization. So *paragraph* is "beside the writing," which fits the marginal-stroke origin exactly. If you carry "graph = diagram" into the etymology, the word stops making sense.
