## Context

A thought experiment about Bitcoin's long-term investment thesis, examining the logical paradox that emerges when you follow both the bull and bear cases to their conclusions. Originally shared on r/buttcoin.

## The paradox

The Bitcoin community's goalposts have shifted from "decentralized and independent asset class for everyone" to a speculative investment vehicle largely tied to its fiat value. This shift creates a paradox where it simply does not matter whether you invest in Bitcoin or not.

**Scenario 1: Bitcoin succeeds.** Bitcoin becomes a broadly adopted institutional and regulated asset class worth $1M+ per BTC. If that happens, institutional buy-in is so prolific that everyone is automatically invested. Bitcoin becomes a backbone of the global finance system, but effectively nothing changes. The same institutions still control the majority of wealth. There is no decoupling from fiat value, so all traditional fiat-based investments are still measured against the same shared value stores, which now just happen to include Bitcoin. Some early holders made a killing, but over time institutional wealth takes over again. Status quo extended.

**Scenario 2: Bitcoin fails.** After another decade of regulatory friction driving market volatility, Bitcoin decouples almost entirely from fiat currency and becomes worthless in fiat terms. The average person no longer has a use case and Bitcoin becomes fringe "internet money" again. Institutional investment ceases. Individual holders face massive losses in fiat terms (though 1 BTC remains 1 BTC).

**The conclusion:** Either Bitcoin becomes universally adopted at the institutional level, in which case you do not need to do anything to become invested, or it fails, in which case you lose nothing by NOT being invested. To quote WOPR: sometimes the best strategy is to simply not play at all.

## The counterweight

Boring investment vehicles tied to assets that physically exist in the world (companies, real estate) continue to be reliable long-term plays. Even if their worth were represented in Bitcoin at the institutional level, a boring index fund portfolio simply migrates over. Highly speculative investment vehicles have always existed. High risk, high reward is a thing, but NOT investing in them is also a sound strategy.
