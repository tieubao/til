## Context

A guide for software developers on how to evaluate potential employers during job interviews. The core philosophy: job interviews are not one-way. Candidates should focus equally on evaluating the company as being evaluated by it. The article provides specific questions to ask engineers, managers, and leadership to uncover how a team actually operates.

**Source:** [A developer's guide to interviewing](https://medium.freecodecamp.com/how-to-interview-as-a-developer-candidate-b666734f12dd)

**Attachment:** [A developer's guide to interviewing.pdf](https://github.com/tieubao/til/files/931696/A.developer.s.guide.to.interviewing.pdf)

## Questions for software engineers

Assess team quality by asking about daily workflows, version control practices, and testing approaches:

- **Development process:** How do engineers know what to work on each day? Consistent answers across the team signal healthy operations. Inconsistent answers signal dysfunction.
- **Quality practices:** Strong teams discuss unit testing thoughtfully, explaining both benefits and limitations rather than following dogma blindly.
- **Tooling and automation:** Modern version control, CI, and collaboration tools indicate investment in developer productivity.
- **Bug management:** Understand their prioritization process. Do they rely on users to find critical issues, or do they catch problems proactively?

## Questions for engineering managers

- **Technical background:** Managers who code understand engineering challenges firsthand.
- **Team health:** Ask about biggest challenges and compare answers with what engineers said. Discrepancies suggest dysfunction.
- **Performance and compensation:** How do raises work? Are they automatic? Do ranking systems pit colleagues against each other?
- **Feedback culture:** Teams conducting retrospectives adapt and improve. Those skipping them may have underlying problems.

## Questions for company leadership

- **Financial health:** Understand funding sources, profitability timelines, and runway.
- **Culture alignment:** Leadership's vision should match what engineers actually experience daily.
- **Outsourcing plans:** Clarify whether roles face future outsourcing risks.

## Practical tips

- Inform interviewers upfront that you will need time for questions.
- Bring a written list of questions. During stressful moments, your brain can shut down.
- Be prepared to ask follow-ups. Inconsistent answers between organizational levels reveal important dysfunction.
- The goal is not finding perfect answers but gathering data to make informed decisions about cultural fit and growth opportunities.
