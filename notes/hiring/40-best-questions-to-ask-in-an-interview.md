## Context

First Round Review compiled 40 high-signal interview questions that candidates should ask employers. The core insight: most candidates waste their question time on surface-level inquiries like "What's the culture like?" Instead, each question should map to your top five deal-breakers. As one expert puts it, "you get so excited that someone likes you, that you lose sight of whether or not you even like them back."

**Source:** [The 40 Best Questions to Ask in an Interview](https://review.firstround.com/the-40-best-questions-to-ask-in-an-interview-how-to-go-deeper-than-whats-the-culture-like)

## Questions by category

**Company growth and strategy:**
- What existential threats keep leadership awake at night?
- What recent failures occurred, and what were the learnings?
- Which financial metrics drive decision-making?
- What do customer wins and losses reveal about market positioning?

**Culture and values:**
- What distinguishes this company from others?
- Has the organization ever prioritized values over revenue?
- How did leadership respond during a major crisis?
- How have employees personally transformed here?

**Employee experience:**
- Can I review your calendar to understand time allocation?
- What traits separate successful employees from those who struggle?
- Why have people voluntarily departed?
- Have employees influenced company changes from the ground up?

**Founder fit (startups):**
- Why are you addressing this specific problem?
- What concerns do investors express about the business?
- What board feedback shaped recent strategic decisions?

**Manager compatibility:**
- How would your team describe your leadership approach?
- What is the most challenging feedback you have given and received?
- How do you develop ICs who do not want management roles?

**Role and responsibilities:**
- What defines failure versus stellar performance in this position?
- What will the first 90 days involve?
- How might this role evolve as the company scales?

**Team dynamics:**
- How does your team navigate competing urgent priorities?
- Tell me about a project that initially derailed but succeeded.
- When did colleagues support each other meaningfully?

## Strategic takeaway

The strongest candidates ask questions that reveal three things: the company's actual health (not marketing speak), the manager's genuine leadership style, and whether organizational values match personal priorities. Red flags include interviewers unable to articulate learnings from failures, or inability to describe positive team interactions. You are evaluating the employer as much as they are evaluating you.
