## Context

Devon Price, a social psychology professor, makes a provocative claim: laziness as a character trait does not exist. When someone appears lazy, there are always situational factors at play that are invisible to the observer. This reframes how we should respond to underperformance, whether in ourselves or in people we manage.

## The core argument

Social psychology research consistently shows that situational constraints predict behavior far better than personality, intelligence, or other individual-level traits. When a student misses a deadline, when a colleague drops the ball, when you yourself cannot get off the couch, the instinct is to blame character. But the evidence points elsewhere: unclear instructions, health issues, competing demands, resource gaps, burnout, trauma.

The label "lazy" is a judgment that closes the door on understanding. It says "this person is defective" instead of "what is blocking this person?"

## Curiosity over judgment

The practical shift is simple but hard to internalize: respond to ineffective behavior with curiosity, not judgment. Ask "what are the situational factors holding this person back?" instead of "why are they so lazy?"

This is especially important when you do not fully understand someone's context. The small annoyances and major traumas that define a person's daily experience are mostly invisible. Without that context, it is easy to impose abstract, rigid expectations and then blame the person when they fall short.

## Implications for management

If you lead people, this reframe changes your default response to underperformance:

- **Investigate before judging.** Look for concrete obstacles: unclear expectations, missing tools, health problems, personal crises.
- **Remove barriers instead of adding pressure.** Shame-based motivation is fragile and temporary. Removing actual obstacles produces durable results.
- **Assume good faith.** Most people want to do well. When they are not performing, something is in the way.

This is not about lowering standards. It is about diagnosing root causes instead of treating symptoms with blame.
