## Context

Guidelines on balancing self-reliance with asking for help, especially when joining a new team or project. From codewithoutrules.com/2017/12/07/asking-for-help/.

## The two failure modes

**Asking too much.** Peppering your lead with a question every 10 minutes interrupts their flow and means you're not spending enough time learning on your own.

**Asking too little.** Avoiding help out of embarrassment leads to spinning your wheels and learning less overall.

## Timeboxing: knowing when to ask

1. When you start a task, ask when it's due.
2. Set a timebox shorter than the deadline (e.g., 3 hours for a 1-day task).
3. When the timebox expires, check your progress. Making headway? Set another. Stuck? Time to ask.

This ensures you're neither too early (you tried first) nor too late (there's still time to finish).

## How to maximize learning from questions

**Always present a potential answer.** Even if it's wrong, it forces you to think, helps the other person understand your mental model, and demonstrates effort.

**Batch your questions.** Write them down as they come up. Wait for a natural break or a few hours, then ask them all at once. You'll often answer some yourself in the meantime.

**Ask open-ended questions, not yes/no.** "Is this right?" yields 1 bit of information. Instead ask:
- "What would a good result look like?"
- "Can you walk me through how this works?"
- "Why is this solution not sufficient?"

## Quick recap

- Do ask for help.
- Batch up your questions.
- Timebox tasks; ask when you hit the box and you're stuck.
- Always provide a potential answer.
- Ask open-ended questions.
