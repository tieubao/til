## Context

Hygge (pronounced "hue-guh", not "hoo-gah") is a Danish word for acknowledging a feeling or moment as cosy, charming, or special. It is not a lifestyle to adopt or a product to buy.

## What hygge is

Hygge requires only consciousness, a certain slowness, and the ability to not just be present but to recognize and enjoy the present. If you don't feel hygge, you're probably not using the word right. It's a feeling, not a thing.

Another definition: "an art of creating intimacy" (with yourself, friends, or your home). English approximations include cosiness, charm, happiness, contentment, security, familiarity, comfort, reassurance, kinship, and simpleness.

## What hygge is not

It doesn't require learning "how to," adopting it as a lifestyle, or buying anything. You can't buy a "hygge living room" and there's no "hygge foods." Anyone selling you hygge either doesn't understand it or is literally trying to sell you something unrelated to the concept.

## Why the Danes created hygge

Danes created hygge to survive boredom, cold, dark, and sameness. With so many harsh, dark days, the simple act of lighting a candle and enjoying a cup of coffee could make a huge difference to one's spirit. Hygge was a way to find moments to celebrate and break up the mundane.

## How it works in practice

By creating simple rituals without effort, the Danes see both domestic and personal life as an art form rather than drudgery to escape from. Examples:

- Brewing real tea with a little china cup every evening
- Stopping at the flower shop every week
- Making coffee a daily ritual rather than a chore
- Lighting a candle with every meal
- A cosy evening in with friends, just enjoying each other's company

Hygge is incorporated into daily life so it becomes a natural extension rather than a forced, stressful event. It's just about being aware of a good moment. The concept is simple; it's only the awareness that seems hard to an outsider.
