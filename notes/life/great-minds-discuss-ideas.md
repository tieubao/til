## Context

An exploration of the Eleanor Roosevelt quote: "Great minds discuss ideas. Average minds discuss events. Small minds discuss people." From personalexcellence.co/blog/great-minds/.

## Three levels of discourse

**Small minds discuss people.** Gossip, tabloid culture, celebrity obsession, office politics, personal attacks. Talking about people as an end in itself is shallow and unproductive.

**Average minds discuss events.** A step up because it's more objective, but still limited. News is filtered through editorial agendas, sensationalized for clicks, and skewed toward negativity. Consuming events without analysis creates an illusion of being informed while painting a fear-based picture of the world. The critical question that gets missed: "What can we do about it?"

**Great minds discuss ideas.** Looking beyond what's immediately visible. Understanding why people do what they do, why problems exist, and what solutions might work. This means digging into root causes, questioning assumptions, and focusing on how to improve things.

## The nuance

People and events are often proxies for discussing ideas. We discuss Elon Musk or Nelson Mandela to understand leadership. We discuss current affairs to learn about the world. Sharing personal events is part of relating to each other.

The problem is when people or events become the endpoint rather than the starting point. Gossip and reactive news consumption don't change lives. Ideas acted upon do.

## Practical shifts

- Dig beneath the surface of every story you encounter
- Stay away from gossip; focus on issues, not people
- Seek out people with knowledgeable opinions and learn from their reasoning
- When an event shocks you, ask: why is this happening? What can be done?
- Focus on solutions, not just reactions
