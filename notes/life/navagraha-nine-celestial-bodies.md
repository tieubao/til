## Context

Reference note on Navagraha, the nine celestial bodies central to Hindu and Vedic astrology. These appear across Hindu temples, calendars, and daily life in Indian culture.

## Definition

**Navagraha** (Sanskrit: nava = "nine", graha = "planet/seizing/holding") refers to the nine heavenly bodies in Hindu cosmology believed to influence human life on Earth. They form the basis of Jyotisha (Hindu astrology) and the Hindu calendar week.

## The nine bodies

| Name | Body | Day | Role |
|------|------|-----|------|
| Surya | Sun | Sunday | Soul, vitality, authority |
| Chandra | Moon | Monday | Mind, emotions, fertility |
| Mangala | Mars | Tuesday | Courage, strength, conflict |
| Budha | Mercury | Wednesday | Intelligence, communication |
| Brihaspati | Jupiter | Thursday | Wisdom, learning, expansion |
| Shukra | Venus | Friday | Love, beauty, prosperity |
| Shani | Saturn | Saturday | Discipline, karma, hardship |
| Rahu | Ascending lunar node | - | Ambition, obsession, illusion |
| Ketu | Descending lunar node | - | Detachment, liberation, past karma |

The first seven correspond directly to the seven days of the week, a pattern shared with Western traditions (Sun-day, Moon-day, etc.). Rahu and Ketu are "shadow planets" representing the lunar nodes where eclipses occur; they have no physical body but hold significant astrological weight.

## Worship and cultural presence

Most Hindu temples maintain a dedicated Navagraha shrine. Devotees perform specific rituals to appease unfavorable planetary influences (doshas). The Carnatic composer Muthuswami Dikshitar (1776-1835) created the *Navagraha Kritis*, devotional compositions honoring each body, combining mantra traditions with Jyotisha knowledge.
