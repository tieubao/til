## Context

An article from codewithoutrules.com about the skill of asking for help as a programmer. Asking too soon feels embarrassing; asking too late wastes time and annoys your manager. The key is timeboxing.

**Source:** [The junior programmer's guide to asking for help at work](https://codewithoutrules.com/2017/12/07/asking-for-help/)

## The two failure modes

1. **Asking too much.** Asking your lead a question every 10 minutes impedes their work and means you are not spending enough time learning on your own.
2. **Asking too little.** Waiting too long or never asking means you spin your wheels and waste time. You also learn less.

## Timeboxing method

When your lead gives you a task, ask how much time you should spend on it. Then set a timebox that is shorter than the deadline:

- Deadline is one day? Set a timebox of three hours.
- After three hours, check your progress. Making headway? Set another timebox. Stuck? Ask for help.
- You have not asked too late (still time to finish) and not too soon (you gave it a real try).

## How to ask well

**Batch your questions.** Write them all down as they occur. When your expert has a free moment, ask them all at once. Chances are you will have figured out some answers in the interim.

**Ask open-ended questions.** "Is this how I do this?" yields 1 bit of information. Instead: "Can you walk me through how this works?" or "What should the result look like?"

**Always present a potential answer.** "My best guess is X because of Y, but I am confused about Z." This forces you to try first, shows effort to your manager, and helps them understand what you know vs. don't know.
