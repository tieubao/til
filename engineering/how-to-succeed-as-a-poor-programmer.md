## Context

A self-described poor programmer shares the heuristics that keep them productive despite not being a strong coder. The premise: awareness of your limitations is more valuable than pretending they do not exist. These heuristics are useful for all programmers but essential for those in the bottom quartile.

## The heuristics

**KISS.** Always ask first: what is the simplest thing that could possibly work? Try that. If it fails, try the second simplest thing.

**YAGNI.** When it comes to features and generality, "You Ain't Gonna Need It." Do not build for hypothetical futures.

**ALAN - Avoid Learning Anything New.** Languages and tools come and go, each requiring a new set of facts, practices, and mentality. Only learn something new when forced. "In 1990 I learned C++. In 2015 I learned Python. Good enough."

**Arrays as the default data structure.** Always ask "how would a FORTRAN programmer do this?" Use trees occasionally, but view them like binge drinking - regular use will damage your liver.

**Never bluff.** Never pretend you understand something when you do not. Google first, then ask people for reading recommendations, then ask for a tutorial from a colleague. We are all ignorant about almost all things.

**Be skeptical of coding advice.** Most coding advice is bad. Think about whether there is empirical evidence that a given piece of advice is true.

**Avoid linking to other software unless forced.** It empirically rarely goes well.

**Develop a comfort zone.** Find some area you are comfortable in and go deep. Do not try to be an expert in everything.

**Let the computer do the work.** Hardware is fast. Software is hard. Embrace the elegance of brute force. If you are bad at programming, you are still programming, something very few people can do.

## Key takeaway

The meta-principle: being honest about your limitations and compensating with simplicity beats pretending you can handle complexity. Bask in the miracle of creation in software. You make something with behavior out of bits.
