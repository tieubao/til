## Context

A manifesto on how ego and parochialism silently destroy engineering organizations. The site argues that role proliferation, the ops/dev divide, and assembly-line thinking all stem from the same root: people protecting territory instead of building shared capability.

**Source:** [Egoless Engineering](https://egoless.engineering/)
**Attachment:** [Egoless Engineering.pdf](https://github.com/user-attachments/files/21316721/Egoless.Engineering.pdf)

## The core problem

Organizations create new job titles for every emerging need, fragmenting responsibilities instead of building capability across teams. Despite claiming to "do DevOps," most companies maintain hard boundaries between operations and development. The portmanteau was supposed to break down divides and build empathy, but it remains mostly theoretical.

Leaders arrange people into narrow specializations, assuming handoffs will improve efficiency. McKinley demonstrates this creates nonlinear bottlenecks: adding engineers without proportional increases in analysis capacity actually slows feature delivery.

**Key insight:** "Poorly factored organizational boundaries create work." When security decisions require ticket queues routed to specialized teams, engineers lose incentive to build secure systems, generating compounding remediation work.

## Domain experts, not domain owners

The most important distinction in the piece: successful teams designate specialists as *experts* tasked with elevating everyone's capability, not *owners* who gatekeep work. Experts spend time teaching. Owners control access.

High-performing teams expected all engineers to participate across disciplines while maintaining expert guidance, rather than siloing frontend, backend, and security work.

## Practices that work

- **Bootcamps:** rotating team assignments expand psychological safety networks
- **Hack weeks:** lower-stakes exploration of adjacent disciplines
- **Shared principles:** explicitly valuing humility over elitism
- **Organizational slack:** 100% utilization kills innovation
- **Reward cooperation explicitly:** counteracts the default drift toward territorialism
- **Model egoless behavior from leadership:** leaders set the tone for whether "doing windows" is acceptable

## The two primal urges

1. **Parochialism** - deference that becomes incuriosity, protective turf-guarding
2. **Ego** - pride that becomes dismissiveness of others' capabilities

Both undermine the cooperation required for execution.

## Final principle

"Misery is a shitty proxy metric for results." Organizations that systematize suffering (mandatory reviews, rigid specialization) mistake constraint for productivity. High performance emerges from trust and curiosity, not friction.
