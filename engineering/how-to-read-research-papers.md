## Context

A guide from UC San Diego's CSE department on reading research papers effectively. Research papers are condensed, assume domain expertise, and may have been assigned for different reasons than they were written, so a structured reading approach is essential.

**Source:** [How to Read an Engineering Research Paper](http://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~wgg/CSE210/howtoread.html)

## Paper structure to expect

Despite their condensed form, papers are repetitive across different levels of detail:
- **Introduction** states motivations and outlines the solution (often enough for experts)
- **Body** details the solution and evaluation
- **Conclusion** recaps contributions and discusses future work

Because of this repetition, reading "out of order" or skipping sections can be productive.

## Eight questions to answer

You are not done reading a paper until you can answer all of these:

1. **What are the motivations?** The "people problem" (real-world benefit) and the "technical problem" (why there is no trivial solution). What previous solutions exist and why are they inadequate?
2. **What is the proposed solution?** The hypothesis or idea. Why should it work better than prior approaches?
3. **How is the solution evaluated?** What argument, implementation, or experiment makes the case? What benefits or problems were identified?
4. **What is your analysis?** Is this a good idea? What flaws do you see? What is most interesting or controversial? Will it actually work in practice?
5. **What are the contributions?** Ideas, software, experimental techniques, area survey?
6. **What are future directions?** Both what the authors identify and what you thought of while reading.
7. **What questions remain?** What would you raise in discussion? What is confusing?
8. **What is your take-away message?** One sentence summary from your perspective.

## Reading process

- Highlight key sentences as you go
- Mark paragraphs by role: motivation, idea, evaluation, contribution
- Write the take-away message on the front page
- Write key questions on the front or near the end
- Try to answer your own questions using other sources
- A second pass is often required before it all comes together

## Takeaway

Active reading with a structured question framework turns passive consumption into deep engagement. The eight questions force you to think critically, not just absorb.
