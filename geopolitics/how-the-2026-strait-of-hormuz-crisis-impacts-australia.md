## Overview

On February 28, 2026, the US and Israel launched joint strikes on Iran, killing Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei. Iran retaliated by closing the Strait of Hormuz to foreign shipping, removing roughly 20% of global oil supply from the market. The IEA has called it the largest supply disruption in the history of the global oil market.

## Transmission mechanism

Australia imports little crude directly from the Middle East. The hit is indirect: 70-80% of Australia's refined petrol and diesel comes via Asian refineries (China, Singapore, South Korea) that depend on Middle Eastern crude. When Iran shut the strait, China restricted fuel exports, cutting off Australia's actual supply chain.

## Consumer impact (as of April 7, 2026)

- National average petrol: A$2.35-2.45/L (down from record highs after excise cut, still +40% vs pre-war)
- Diesel: A$3.13/L, highest on record since 2006
- 312+ service stations reporting diesel outages, NSW hardest hit (150-182 stations dry)
- Construction firms adding 8-10% fuel surcharges to contracts
- Jet fuel prices doubled, airlines flagging 5-10% summer cancellations
- Young Australians in regional areas reconsidering jobs due to commute costs

## Structural vulnerability

Australia holds only 29-39 days of fuel reserves vs the IEA's 90-day benchmark. It has not met this benchmark since 2012. The country is the only IEA member that does not hold the mandatory 90-day reserve.

The supply chain runs on a "just in time" philosophy with almost no buffer. Limited domestic refining capacity eliminates redundancy. Transit times from Persian Gulf refineries to Australian ports range 25-35 days.

## Government response (April 1, 2026)

- Fuel excise halved for 3 months (saving 26.3 cents/L, Apr 1 to Jun 30)
- 20% of petrol and diesel reserves released, targeted at regional areas
- Supply agreement secured with Singapore
- ACCC tasked with fuel price monitoring and on-the-spot fines for gouging
- Heavy Vehicle Road User Charge reduced to zero for 3 months
- PM Albanese urged citizens to use public transport to conserve fuel for critical industries
- Fuel quality standards temporarily lowered for 60 days (higher-sulphur fuel allowed)

## Military posture

Australia declined to join Trump's proposed multinational naval coalition for the Strait of Hormuz. On March 16, a government minister explicitly stated Australia would not send ships. Japan, Germany, Spain, Italy, and the UK also took cautious approaches.

## Macro outlook

- Westpac forecasts headline inflation could reach 5.5% year-on-year by mid-2026
- RBA has hiked interest rates twice (now at 4.10%)
- Food prices rising: beef/veal +13.5%, lamb/goat +13.4% annually
- Economists forecast fuel prices remaining 20-40% above early 2026 levels for at least 6 months
- If excise cut expires on Jul 1 without extension, petrol jumps ~26 cents/L overnight
