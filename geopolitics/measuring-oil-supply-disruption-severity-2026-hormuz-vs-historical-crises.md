There is no single universal "Richter scale" for oil supply disruptions, but analysts measure severity across three key dimensions: volume lost (mb/d removed from market), price impact (percentage surge in benchmark crude), and duration. The IEA's emergency reserve activation serves as the de facto severity indicator, having been triggered only 6 times since 1974.

|--------|--------------------|--------------------|---------------------|
| 1973 Arab embargo | 4.5 | +300% ($3 to $12) | N/A (IEA founded after) |
| 1979 Iran revolution | 5.0 | +186% ($14 to $40) | N/A |
| 1980 Iran-Iraq war | ~4.0 | Sustained high | N/A |
| 1990 Gulf war | ~4.3 | Spike then retreat | ~33 mb |
| 2011 Libya | ~1.5 | Moderate | ~60 mb |
| 2022 Russia-Ukraine | ~3.0 | +64% ($78 to $128) | ~240 mb |
| 2026 Hormuz | 10-15 | +91% ($66 to $126) | 400 mb (largest ever) |

## The IEA assessment

IEA chief Fatih Birol stated the current crisis has removed 12 million barrels per day, which is "more than two of" the 1973 and 1979 crises combined (each removed ~5 mb/d). Gas supply losses also exceed the Russia-Ukraine disruption. He called it "the biggest in history" and warned that April will be "much worse than March" because ships already in transit before the war have now all arrived.

## Counterpoint: context matters

The global oil market today exceeds 104 mb/d total supply, so even 10-15 mb/d lost is a smaller percentage than the 1973 shock was relative to the market at the time. Oil prices at ~$100-110/barrel remain well below previous crisis peaks in real (inflation-adjusted) terms. The 1973 embargo caused a 300% price spike; 2026 has hit about 91% so far. Strategic reserves, alternative routing (Saudi East-West pipeline, UAE Abu Dhabi-Fujairah line), and some selective Iranian transit for "non-hostile" vessels have partially cushioned the blow.

## Academic measurement approaches

Huntington (2018) found that a 10% cut in world oil supplies from OPEC/Gulf disruptions historically correlates with a 35-43% increase in inflation-adjusted crude prices. Oil Vulnerability Indices (OVI) use composite indicators including import dependency ratios, GDP exposure, route concentration, and reserve levels. The Stanford Energy Modeling Forum has assessed disruption risks using expert elicitation with probability distributions across supply regions.

## Duration risk

The Dallas Fed modeled that a one-quarter closure would raise WTI to $98/barrel and lower global GDP growth by 2.9 percentage points. A three-quarter disruption could reduce Q4-over-Q4 global GDP growth by 1.3 percentage points. As of early April, ceasefire negotiations are ongoing but Iran has rejected US terms, and the strait remains effectively closed with selective passage only.
