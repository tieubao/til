## Context

Morgan Housel's curated collection of psychological, social, and behavioral concepts that explain how the world actually works, often contradicting our intuitions. Not a single argument but a reference card of mental models.

## Decision-making and bias

**Base-Rate Neglect.** People overestimate their uniqueness while ignoring historical success rates. Your startup isn't special just because you believe in it; the base rate of startup failure still applies.

**Buridan's Ass.** Decision paralysis when options appear equally viable. The irony: the harder the decision (because options are so close), the less the choice actually matters.

**Fredkin's Paradox.** A formalization of the above: the more equally attractive two options are, the harder the decision feels, yet the less consequential it is. Just pick one.

## Social dynamics

**Cumulative Advantage.** Early success breeds access to more opportunities; early failure closes doors. Small initial differences compound into large gaps over time.

**Three Men Make a Tiger.** Belief is driven by repetition, not truth. If enough people repeat something, it becomes accepted fact regardless of evidence.

**Abilene Paradox.** Groups pursue actions nobody actually wants because each member fears being the lone dissenter. Everyone goes along, assuming others are enthusiastic.

## Information and perception

**Anscombe's Quartet.** Datasets can look identical statistically yet tell completely different stories visually. Exact calculations don't always offer good representation of reality.

**Baader-Meinhof Phenomenon.** Once you learn a new concept, you start seeing it everywhere. This inflates your sense of how common or important it is.

**Bizarreness Effect.** Unusual events are disproportionately memorable, distorting our model of what's normal. The news exploits this constantly.

## System-level problems

**Cobra Effect.** When solutions create incentives that worsen the original problem. Named after a bounty program for cobras that led people to breed them.

**Braess's Paradox.** Adding resources can reduce overall efficiency. Building more roads can increase traffic because of changed routing behavior.

**Feedback Loops.** Self-reinforcing cycles amplify both gains and losses. Understanding whether you're in a positive or negative loop matters more than understanding any single event.

## Other standouts

**Knightian Uncertainty.** Some risks are literally unmeasurable. Acknowledging this is wisdom, not weakness.

**Fluency Heuristic.** Ideas stated clearly and confidently sound more true, regardless of content. Presentation skills can make nonsense convincing.

**Denomination Effect.** People spend small bills more freely than large ones of equal total value. Framing changes behavior even when the math is identical.

## Key takeaway

This collection works best as a periodic re-read. Each concept is a lens; having many lenses available helps you recognize patterns faster. The meta-lesson: the world runs on psychology and incentives more than logic and intentions.
