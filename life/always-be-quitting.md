## Context

A philosophy for working in engineering teams that sounds counterintuitive: act as if you might leave on short notice. Not because you want to quit, but because this mindset forces behaviors that make you a better engineer, a better teammate, and paradoxically more promotable.

## The core paradox

Being indispensable sounds like job security, but it is actually a trap. If you are the only person who knows how something works, you cannot take vacations, you cannot rotate to new projects, and you cannot grow into higher-level roles. When the project eventually becomes irrelevant, so does your position. Disposability is freedom.

## The 10 practices

1. **Document your knowledge.** Every question someone asks you reveals a documentation gap. Write the answer down so the next person does not need you.

2. **Document long-term plans.** People should understand what is coming by reading plans, not by asking you in real time. Plan a few months ahead.

3. **Document meetings.** Keep public notes for all meetings: who attended, what was discussed, conclusions reached. Your replacement will need these.

4. **Bring others to meetings.** If you are the only person from your team in a meeting, involve someone else. Avoid becoming the sole point of contact.

5. **Train people around you.** The goal is their independence. Familiarize them with plans, technologies, and documentation.

6. **Identify and train your replacement.** To switch roles, you need someone who can take over. Actively coach them.

7. **Give power to the people.** Let them make their own choices. Guide them with the right data instead of requiring them to ask permission.

8. **Do not make yourself the point of contact.** Establish mailing lists and group channels that can accommodate others, then grow those groups.

9. **Delegate.** Once others are included, informed, and empowered, delegate work that helps them grow. Focus on what only you can do.

10. **Always be learning.** Use freed-up bandwidth to grow your knowledge in areas aligned with where you want to go next.

## Why this is not abdication

Nothing here means dropping responsibility. You are still accountable for your projects and teams. The difference is that your accountability now rests on systems and people rather than on your personal presence. This is exactly what distinguishes senior/staff-level engineers from mid-level ones: they make the whole team better, not just themselves.
