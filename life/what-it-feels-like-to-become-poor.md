## Context

A Quora answer (25,000+ upvotes, 1M+ views) from a luxury goods consultant who lost everything in the 2008 financial crisis. A story about ego, humility, and finding salvation in the last place you would look.

## The fall

The author was a high-profile luxury goods and hospitality consultant working with high-net-worth clients. When Lehman Brothers folded, his consulting contract was not renewed. Tenants defaulted on rent. He kept paying mortgages. He lost roughly $3 million in real estate equity and a couple hundred thousand in cash.

He spent over a year looking for work. Consulting, sales, waiting tables, bartending, limo driving, grocery bagging. Nothing. "I had the stink of failure all over me."

## The car wash

A friend offered him a job at his car wash. Outdoor work, taking orders in winter in Colorado. He declined, twice.

The reason was his father. Every good father has a catch phrase to motivate sons to do better. His father's was the car wash. After Little League they would drive through Towne Auto Wash and his father would point to the guy handing out tickets: "You don't want to end up like that guy, do you?"

Working in a car wash was the ultimate admission of failure. Not losing assets, not selling watches and cars. The car wash.

But reality kicked in when he did not have money for a niece's birthday present. He called his friend back.

## The turning point

On his third day, his former assistant pulled in driving a brand new Lexus. Now his humiliation was complete. But she smiled, jumped out, ran over and gave him a hug. She told him she was happy to see him working, that he had been a great boss, and that many of her friends were unemployed.

"I realized I'd been beating myself up needlessly."

He decided to use all his skills to increase his friend's business. Something unexpected happened:

- He saw hundreds of people daily. None thought he was a failure.
- He smiled. They smiled back. He sold "a gazillion deluxe washes."
- Dozens of people he knew, old clients, friends, all said something positive.
- They held him in higher esteem for doing what had to be done.
- A local magazine wrote an article about him.
- His confidence came back.

"There's no way to spin a story when you are asking people if they want the basic or deluxe wash. There's no amount of charm or polish or bullshit that can hide the truth. I was working in a car wash, and nobody thought I was a failure. Not even my father."

## The comeback

Six months later, an old client called. He needed help setting up a luxury club. The author approached the new contract with vigor he had not felt in years. Another contract took him to Asia, where he has been consulting ever since.

His worst fear turned out to be his salvation. It gave him confidence, paid his bills, and positioned him to move his company to Asia with access to new cultures and growing markets.

"I have a red nylon jacket with a car wash logo on it that reminds me that for my version of success, I don't have to be."
