## Context

Chris Dixon's 2016 essay arguing that the computing industry follows two independent cycles: financial cycles (unpredictable market fluctuations) and product cycles (the actual technological driver). Major computing eras emerge approximately every 10-15 years.

**Source:** [What's Next in Computing?](https://medium.com/@cdixon/what-s-next-in-computing-e54b870b80cc)

"The business plans of the next 10,000 startups are easy to forecast: Take X and add AI. This is a big deal, and now it's here." - Kevin Kelly

## Computing eras

Each era progresses through two phases: gestation (expensive, incomplete) and growth (breakthrough products enabling exponential adoption).

| Era | Gestation | Growth trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Mainframe | 1950s-60s | Large organizations only |
| PC | 1970s | IBM PC (1981) |
| Internet | 1980s-early 90s | Mosaic browser (1993) |
| Mobile | 2000s | iPhone/Android (2007) |
| Hardware+AI | 2010s | Multiple simultaneous platforms |

## The next era: hardware + AI integration

**Hardware trends:** The "peace dividend of the smartphone war" repurposes mobile components into drones, IoT, and wearables. Systems-on-chip reduce basic computing costs from ~$100 to ~$10. Processors and sensors are becoming dramatically smaller and cheaper.

**Software revolution:** Machine learning is a "core, transformative way" organizations are reimagining operations. Deep learning surpassed human performance in image recognition. Open-source tools like TensorFlow democratize access.

**Emerging platforms (in gestation as of 2016):**
- Autonomous vehicles
- Drones with computer vision
- Internet of Things
- Wearables and voice interfaces
- Virtual and Augmented Reality

## Key insight

Multiple new computing eras are arriving simultaneously, not just one. AI-enhanced smart devices embed intelligence into physical objects across all these platforms.
