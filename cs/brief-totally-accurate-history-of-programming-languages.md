## Context

A satirical timeline of programming language development, originally by Casper von B. on Medium. Humorous reimaginings of key moments in CS history, crediting James Iry's 2009 similar work.

**Source:** [A Brief Totally Accurate History of Programming Languages](https://medium.com/@caspervonb/a-brief-totally-accurate-history-of-programming-languages-cd93ec806124)

## Selected highlights

**1800** - Joseph Marie Jacquard teaches a loom to read punch cards, creating "the first heavily multi-threaded processing unit."

**1842** - Ada Lovelace writes the first computer program. The catch: computers don't exist yet.

**1936** - Alan Turing and Alonzo Church both "invent everything." Turing faces historical tragedy while Church works undisturbed at Princeton.

**1957** - FORTRAN becomes "the first language that real programmers use."

**1972** - Dennis Ritchie creates C at Bell Labs during work hours, then uses the remaining time to invent Unix as a "demonstration program."

**1987** - Larry Wall has a "religious experience" and makes Perl "the doctrine."

**1996** - Java emerges as "overly verbose," spawning design patterns that prioritize complexity.

**2010** - Rust's creation immediately triggers demands to "rewrite every single piece of software."

**2012** - TypeScript becomes "JavaScript but with more Java in it."

## Why it's worth keeping

Humor aside, the timeline is a useful mnemonic for when major languages appeared and the cultural context around each. The jokes land because they capture real community attitudes.
