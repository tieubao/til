## Context

Cobie's essay challenges the popular narrative that venture capitalists uniquely exploit retail investors in crypto. The piece argues that the real issue is understanding incentive structures across all market participants, not singling out VCs.

## The VC spectrum

Cobie identifies two ends of the crypto VC landscape:

- **Long-term builders** who maintain reputations and hold positions over decades. They need founder relationships and retail trust.
- **Short-term mercenaries** focused on quick profits from bull markets. They can dump tokens without consequence because they have no long-term reputation to protect.

The determining factor is time horizon. A twenty-year investor behaves fundamentally differently from a two-year investor.

## Predatory tokenomics

Bull markets enable founders to create "free money" deals with locked tokens and massive airdrops that favor early investors. This attracts mercenary capital while making legitimate fundraising harder. The cycle reinforces itself: easy money draws in bad actors, which erodes trust, which makes it harder for good actors to raise.

## The (3,3) problem

Crypto communities adopt game-theory language ("wagmi," "hodl," "(3,3)") that suggests mutual benefit through cooperation. The (3,3) notation comes from OlympusDAO's game theory framing where staking (cooperating) produces the best outcome for all participants. But individual actors - anons, YouTubers, founders - often prioritize personal profits over community welfare. The rhetoric of cooperation masks the reality of defection.

## Key insight

"Show me the incentive and I will show you the outcome." The system is not uniquely rigged against retail. All participants face identical adversarial incentives. Reputations, time horizons, and financial alignment determine behavior far more than rhetoric about decentralization or community. Understanding this is the actual edge in crypto investing.
