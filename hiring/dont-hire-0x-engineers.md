## Context

Zach Holman challenges the tech industry's obsession with hiring "10x engineers" and "A-players." His argument: rather than pursuing an impossible ideal, companies should focus on practical hiring - finding capable people who do not suck, rather than exclusively hunting top-tier mythical talent.

**Source:** [Just Don't Hire 0x Engineers](https://zachholman.com/posts/0x-engineers/)

## The argument

**Pain avoidance drives unrealistic standards.** Using a philosophy thought experiment, Holman observes that imagining Hell is easier than imagining Heaven. Similarly, companies fixate on avoiding bad hires rather than positively defining what great hiring looks like. This "pain avoidance" mindset produces unrealistic expectations about who is actually available and affordable.

**Reality versus marketing.** While most companies claim to hire exclusively top performers, Holman's experience at Gild (a hiring analytics platform) revealed the truth: most organizations actually wanted competent people they could afford. Companies use aspirational language about excellence while actually seeking reliability within budget constraints.

**The baseball analogy.** Well-coordinated average teams often outperform collections of individual superstars. Organizational fit and teamwork matter more than hunting exclusively for exceptional individuals.

**Excellence is not universal.** Not every project requires perfection. Sometimes "quick" outperforms "best," and "flexible" beats "optimized." Products and teams do not require constant excellence across all dimensions.

## Practical implications

- Accept that most organizations are average, and that is acceptable.
- Define realistic hiring needs based on budget and role requirements.
- Recognize that good teamwork often matters more than individual brilliance.
- Stop setting impossible expectations that damage industry hiring culture.
- Match hiring standards to actual business needs, not aspirational marketing copy.
- The 0x engineer (someone who produces nothing or causes net negative output) is the real problem to avoid, not the absence of 10x engineers.
