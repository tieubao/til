## Context

Lori Goler, Facebook's Head of People, describes the three key factors Facebook looks for in every candidate regardless of level, team, or location. Written in 2017 when Facebook had grown from a few hundred employees to 17,000 across 50 offices in 30 countries.

**Source:** [I'm Facebook's Head of People - Here's What We're Hiring For Right Now (And Why)](https://www.fastcompany.com/40408179/im-facebooks-head-of-people-heres-what-were-hiring-for-right-now-and-why)

## The three hiring factors

**1. Your strengths (well proven or otherwise)**

People in jobs that play to their talents and let them do work they enjoy are more engaged and perform better. Goler's go-to question: "What were you doing on your very best day at work?" followed by a hint: "It was probably a day when you lost track of time because you were so engrossed in your work." The goal is to match people to roles where that flow state happens daily, not occasionally.

This matters because some recruiting experts argue past performance is not a reliable indicator of future success, and that hiring for potential is often smarter than hiring for performance. In both cases, focusing on strengths is the right starting point.

**2. The skills to build it yourself**

Builders look at the world with fresh eyes. They see things that are good but could be better, and figure out how to improve them. Zuckerberg himself still codes; he built an AI system for his own home in 2016. Facebook looks for this builder mindset at every level.

The signal they look for: "If you can show a hiring manager something you yourself thought of, put together on your own, and then convinced other people to start using, you'll stand a better chance of sticking out." This applies whether the candidate is applying for an executive role or an internship.

**3. Comfort with learning the hard way**

Tomorrow's technology depends on imagination, which demands genuine intellectual curiosity, not just a good GPA. Facebook looks for a steep learning curve rather than a constant stream of successes. Have you tried something really hard and failed the first, second, maybe third time, and learned from it?

The key message to candidates: "Don't hesitate to talk about the blunders you've made in the pursuit of big ideas. We want to hear about those as much as your wins." You cannot iterate on new platforms, AI, or VR without a deep love of learning and the resilience to push through stumbles.

## Practical takeaway

When interviewing, be prepared to answer three questions: What are you good at? What have you tried to make? And what did you learn from the experience, no matter how it went?
