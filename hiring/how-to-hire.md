## Context

A comprehensive guide to hiring philosophy, arguing that most companies get hiring wrong by optimizing for the wrong signals. The article covers six core principles for building effective hiring practices, with a strong emphasis on strengths-based evaluation and trajectory over experience.

**Source:** [How to Hire](https://medium.com/swlh/how-to-hire-34f4ded5f176)

## Six hiring principles

**1. Hire for strengths, not weakness-absence.** Stop committee voting - it eliminates high conviction in favor of bland consensus. Have hiring managers decide while interview teams identify what candidates excel at. One amazing skill often translates across domains.

**2. Prioritize trajectory over experience.** Future growth potential matters more than past accomplishments. Look for candidates who discuss possibilities rather than only achievements. Someone's willingness to learn outweighs where they have been.

**3. Seek doers, not talkers.** Interview ability does not predict job performance. Test candidates practically: ask engineers to code, salespeople to sell. "The most common hiring mistake is hiring good interviewers."

**4. Value learners over fixed experts.** Expertise matters, but rapid organizational change makes static knowledge obsolete. Hire curious people who ask questions and adapt. Curiosity signals learning capacity.

**5. Hire differently.** Natural bias pulls toward hiring similar people. Fight this instinct. Diverse backgrounds bring new perspectives and creativity. Different hires initially seem odd but ultimately expand organizational capability.

**6. Always reject ego.** Distinguish between confidence (strong) and ego (weak). Humble high performers make others better. Egotistical people create destructive competition. "The truly great use their greatness to make those around them greater."

## Key framing ideas

**Hiring as failure recognition.** Every hire acknowledges the team could not scale alone. Rather than celebrating headcount growth, treat each new role as overhead that must justify itself.

**Power law distribution.** Top performers create dramatically more value than average employees - potentially 20x more in high-leverage startups.

**False positives over false negatives.** Bad hires can be corrected quickly, but rejected candidates who would have succeeded represent permanent, unknowable losses. Facebook passing on WhatsApp's Brian Acton is the canonical example. Embrace hiring risk; fear missing exceptional talent.

**Dynamic culture building.** Hire people who expand your culture, not just fit it. New employees will outnumber founders and define future culture. "Hire culture-contributors who will make our culture better."
