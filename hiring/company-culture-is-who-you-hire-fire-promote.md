## Context

This note captures the core argument from a widely-shared Medium article: company culture is not what you write on the wall or put in your handbook. Culture is defined by three actions - who you hire, who you fire, and who you promote. These decisions reveal what the organization truly values, regardless of stated values.

**Attachment:** [Your Company's Culture is Who You Hire, Fire, & Promote.pdf](https://github.com/tieubao/til/files/1167586/Your.Company.s.Culture.is.Who.You.Hire.Fire.Promote.pdf)
**Attachment:** [Your Company Culture Is Who You Hire, Fire & Promote - Medium.pdf](https://github.com/tieubao/til/files/1167587/Your.Company.Culture.Is.Who.You.Hire.Fire.Promote.Medium.pdf)

## The argument

**Culture is revealed through behavior, not declarations.** Every company claims to value innovation, collaboration, and integrity. But culture is actually defined by which behaviors get rewarded and which get punished. The three highest-signal decisions are:

**Who you hire** - The qualities you select for in candidates become the qualities that define your team. If you hire only for technical skill and ignore character, you are building a culture that tolerates bad character. If you hire for culture fit without defining what that means, you are building a culture of conformity.

**Who you fire** - This is where stated values meet reality. When a high performer behaves toxically and faces no consequences, the message is clear: performance excuses everything. When someone is let go for violating values despite good output, it signals that values are real constraints, not decoration.

**Who you promote** - Promotion is the loudest signal a company sends about what it values. Promoting the person who ships fast but burns bridges tells everyone that speed matters more than collaboration. Promoting the person who mentors others and builds consensus tells a different story entirely.

## Implications

- Culture audits should examine hiring criteria, firing patterns, and promotion decisions rather than mission statements.
- Leaders who tolerate toxic high performers are actively choosing that as their culture.
- The gap between stated culture and actual culture is measurable by looking at these three decisions.
- Changing culture requires changing who gets hired, who gets consequences, and who gets promoted. Everything else is theater.
