## Context

Donn Felker describes two complementary processes for hiring software developers: one for full-time programmers and one for outsourced contractors. Both follow a systematic, multi-stage approach that emphasizes practical coding assessment over credentials. The key insight shared across both: the programming challenge is the great equalizer, and over 60% of candidates will never finish it.

**Source:** [How to Hire Programmers](http://www.donnfelker.com/how-to-hire-programmers/)
**Source:** [How to Hire Outsourced Developers](http://www.donnfelker.com/how-to-hire-outsourced-developers/)

## Hiring full-time programmers: 4-step process

**1. Informal HR screen (10-30 min)** - Assess basic communication skills and cultural compatibility through casual conversation. If they fail here, stop.

**2. Technical phone screen (10-30 min)** - Evaluate technical knowledge via open-ended questions specific to your tech stack. Ask candidates to explain why they made specific choices, not just what they know.

**3. Programming challenge (2-4 hours)** - Have candidates solve a real coding problem. This stage separates genuine talent from those who merely talk a good game. Over 60% of candidates never finish. Of those who do, only about 20% complete it correctly.

**4. Culture fit evaluation (2-4 hours)** - Bring the candidate in-person to meet the team. Take them to regular team lunch spots rather than fancy restaurants. This reveals how they interact in natural environments. Felker shares an example of rejecting an otherwise qualified candidate due to rudeness uncovered during the meal.

## Hiring outsourced developers: 4-step process

**1. Post a clear job description** - Use accurate titles ("Senior Rails Developer" not "Rails Badass"). Set English proficiency requirement to 5/5. Specify payment structure clearly.

**2. Preliminary screening** - From 25-60 applicants, filter by communication quality, required skills, and budget alignment. Narrow to 10+ qualified candidates and hire them for a paid one-hour task. Roughly 50% proceed.

**3. Programming challenge** - Assign a real but manageable coding task completable in one hour by experienced developers. Post on GitHub so candidates submit pull requests. This reveals coding ability, communication, following directions, Git knowledge, and commitment to deadlines.

**4. Select your contractor** - After code review, hire your top candidate. Notify runners-up that you may contact them for future opportunities.

## Key principles

- Budget $50-$250 to find the right outsourced developer. Asking for unpaid work eliminates all quality candidates immediately.
- Space interview steps 4-6 hours apart so teams can make deliberate advancement decisions.
- If you cannot code, engage a programmer friend for 2 hours to review submissions using a No/Maybe/Yes scale.
- "It's better to wait for the right candidate than to have a bad hire."
