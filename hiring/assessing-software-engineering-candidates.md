## Context

Bryan Cantrill outlines a comprehensive framework for evaluating software engineering candidates. The core argument: in-person interviewing alone is unreliable and selects for surface personality traits. As much assessment as possible should be done by letting candidates show themselves as engineers truly work - on their own, in writing.

**Source:** [Assessing software engineering candidates](http://dtrace.org/blogs/bmc/2018/10/05/assessing-software-engineering-candidates/)

## Five traits to evaluate

**1. Aptitude** - Assessed through written artifacts, not pop quizzes. Candidates submit three samples:
- **Code sample:** Code they are proud of, or something they built specifically (8-24 hours of work over two weeks max).
- **Writing sample:** Technical writing - block comments, blog entries, architectural docs, or mailing list comments.
- **Analysis sample:** Written analysis of software behavior, e.g., a debugging war story written down thoughtfully.

**2. Education** - Both formal and informal. Completion of formal education matters more than the institution. The failure to complete may indicate deeper problems, but life circumstances must be considered. For informal education, probe with: "What is something you learned that was a struggle for you?"

**3. Motivation** - Often not assessed but dictates everything. Look for intrinsic motivation. Open source contribution is a strong signal.

**4. Values** - Reflect tradeoffs engineers make daily (speed vs. rigor). Probe through questions about pride, regret, happiness, unhappiness, mentors, and admired qualities. Written answers first, then conversation.

**5. Integrity** - Criminal background checks verify identity. Credential checks catch "fudging" of degrees, grades, employment dates. References help but are fallible since manipulative people can fool references too.

## Interview mechanics

**Pre-interview:** Everyone on the schedule reads all candidate-submitted materials. A pre-meeting discusses approach and open questions.

**Job talk (senior candidates, 7+ years):** A 30-minute technical presentation on anything they have worked on. This reveals teaching ability, technical mastery, and how they field questions.

**The post-interview meeting:** Each interviewer arrives with one of three positions - Hire, Do not hire, or Insufficient information. The meeting starts with "Do not hire" positions to ensure reservations are not silenced. A single "Do not hire" on values or integrity should raise serious doubts.

## Key insights

- Writing good code and writing good prose are found together in the most capable engineers.
- The most accurate way to assess integrity is for the candidate to already be trusted by someone you trust.
- Assess the assessment process by asking candidates for their perspective on it. They are evaluating many hiring processes concurrently.
- A thoughtful process encourages candidate candor, which in turn strengthens the process.
