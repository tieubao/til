## Context

Steve Blank draws parallels between Tesla/Elon Musk and GM's history of William Durant (founder) vs Alfred Sloan (professional manager). The article explores the recurring tension between visionary founders and operational leaders.

**Source:** [To Understand the Future of Tesla, Look to the History of GM](https://hbr.org/2018/04/to-understand-the-future-of-tesla-look-to-the-history-of-gm)

## The GM story

**William Durant (founder, 1904):** A visionary entrepreneur who built GM into a $10 billion enterprise (in today's dollars) but struggled with execution. He was eventually fired by the board.

**Alfred P. Sloan (professional manager, 1923-1956):** Brought in to professionalize GM's operations. Sloan put in place planning and strategy, measurements, and the principles of decentralization. He invented the idea of the modern corporation.

## The pattern

Visionary founders have exceptional foresight about industry futures but struggle with operational focus. During downturns, Durant's one-man show was damaging the company. He couldn't prioritize, couldn't find time to meet with his direct reports.

Professional managers implement systematic financial controls, divisional organization, and strategic planning, creating sustainable competitive advantage through execution rather than vision.

## The Tesla parallel

Musk's divided attention across Tesla, SpaceX, Boring Company, and OpenAI mirrors Durant's unfocused expansion. The question: is Musk more like Durant or Sloan? Blank suggests the $2.6 billion compensation scheme might be better spent finding Tesla's equivalent to Sloan.

## The transferable insight

Every startup eventually faces this transition: from the visionary who sees the future to the operator who can build the machine to get there. The founder who can do both is rare. Most companies need to make this handoff, and getting the timing right is critical.
