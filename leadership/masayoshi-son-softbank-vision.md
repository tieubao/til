## Context

A condensed profile of Masayoshi Son from a Fast Company article (2018). Son created the $100 billion Vision Fund to back AI-driven startups, betting that computers will run the planet more intelligently than humans. At the time, this was the largest venture fund ever created, dwarfing the entire VC industry's annual investment of ~$70 billion.

## Origin story

Son grew up poor on the island of Kyushu, Japan. His family emigrated from Korea in the 1960s during a time of intense anti-foreign sentiment. His parents named him Masayoshi ("justice") hoping it would deflect prejudice. It did not; he was bullied at school.

His father Mitsunori told him, "I believe you are a genius. You just don't know your destiny yet." Young Son helped his father's struggling coffee shop by suggesting they give away free coffee to attract customers, then make money once they came through the door. He later used the same tactic (free modems on the street) to build SoftBank.

After UC Berkeley, he launched SoftBank in 1981 with two part-time employees and a 50-year plan. When he told them he would have $75 million in sales in five years, both quit.

## Investment philosophy

Son does not focus on profit margins during pitch meetings. His question: "How fast can the company go?" He tells founders, "The entrepreneur's ambition is the only cap to the company's potential."

His strategy, called **gun-senryaku** (a flock of birds flying in formation), connects portfolio companies to help each other grow. Compass and Uber rent space from WeWork. Mapbox works with Uber. The "family concept" creates trust and shared purpose among portfolio companies.

Key investments: $20M in Alibaba (became worth $50B at IPO), $32B acquisition of Arm, billions into WeWork, Uber, ByteDance, and dozens of AI startups.

## Resilience through catastrophe

During the dotcom crash (2000-2001), SoftBank's stock fell 90% and $70 billion of Son's net worth disappeared. He tripled his exposure during the crash, betting on 600 internet companies. "Most human beings who've had those experiences become tentative," a colleague observed. "You've never seen someone so fearless."

Even while his empire was tanking, he invested $20M in an obscure Chinese e-commerce site run by a former teacher. That was Alibaba.

## The Vision Fund thesis

Son believes AI will cross into every industry. The Vision Fund's minimum investment is $100M. In two years it committed $70B+ across transportation, real estate, food, logistics, and more.

The $100B fund was anchored by Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund ($45B). This created a geopolitical problem when the Khashoggi murder occurred in 2018, but Son pragmatically maintained the relationship: "He is a businessman. He is not going to turn his back on $45 billion."

## What makes Son different

He has a "300-year plan." His childhood hero is Sakamoto Ryoma, a ronin who overthrew feudal Japan and launched the country into the modern age. He keeps a portrait in his office: "Every morning it reminds me to make a decision worthy of Ryoma."

He is small, soft-spoken, self-deprecating. When friends teased him about resembling Charlie Brown, he put a Snoopy doll on his desk. He loves Star Wars and quotes Yoda on how he makes investment picks.

His colleagues say he is happiest when chatting with startup founders, brainstorming, strategizing, inventing. "If Masa could spend the whole day doing what he loves, it would be meeting with entrepreneurs."
