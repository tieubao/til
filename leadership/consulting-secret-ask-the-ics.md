## Context

A blog post by Glyph (of Twisted Python fame) on the "secret technique" used by experienced software consultants. Companies call for outside help when things are going poorly, and the root cause is almost always a people problem disguised as a technology problem.

**Source:** [Let Me Tell You A Secret](https://blog.glyph.im/2024/02/let-me-tell-you-a-secret.html)

**Attachment:** [Deciphering Glyph - Let Me Tell You A Secret.pdf](https://github.com/user-attachments/files/15816691/Deciphering.Glyph.Let.Me.Tell.You.A.Secret.pdf)

## The technique

Schedule a 90-minute meeting with the most senior IC engineer on the team. Bring a pen and paper. Ask one question: "What is broken about this place?" Then write down everything. 90 minutes is typically not enough. One week later, read a lightly edited transcript to executive leadership. This is routinely praised as keen strategic insight.

## Why it works

Senior ICs know exactly what is wrong. They live in the codebase and the processes every day. The information already exists inside the organization; the problem is that it never reaches leadership in unfiltered form.

## Rules for the listening session

- **Only listen.** Do not push back, even if you feel defensive. Process that feeling later.
- **Do not commit to fixing anything** during the meeting. ICs do not have the full picture on priorities. Take their concerns as feedback for improving internal comms, not as direct action items.
- **Do not make empty promises.** Acknowledge what you heard, then act deliberately.

## Why leaders should do this themselves

A consultant is seen as "some random guy wasting their time with a meeting." But if leadership does this directly, they gain credibility. People remember when they personally told you what was wrong and you took it seriously. Active listening alone can improve morale. If you then make meaningful changes, you inspire genuine respect.
