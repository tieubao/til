## Context

Viktor Petersson shares lessons from ten years of working and running remote teams. Written in 2019, these insights remain relevant for any organization considering or running remote-first operations.

**Source:** [A decade of remote work](https://blog.viktorpetersson.com/2019/05/18/a-decade-of-remote.html)

## Remote is not for everyone

Some people excel remotely; others work better in an office. People who fail at remote work tend to either lack self-discipline or are socially oriented and thrive being around others. Younger workers (early 20s) tend to struggle more than those in their late 20s and 30s. As a manager, watch for this during the first year.

## Either you're remote or you're not

Remote-only or no remote at all. Half-measures favor office workers because of information flow. Water cooler chats, coffee breaks, and after-work drinks create unevenly distributed information. Remote-only cultures force information into official channels (email, chat, GitHub Issues), making communication more organized and equitable.

## Company summits matter

Even for remote teams, meeting in person every year (or twice a year) makes a huge difference. Text-based chat is low context, making it easy to misread intent. After meeting someone in person, you have more context to interpret their messages. Petersson recommends quarterly developer summits and annual company-wide summits.

## Hiring remote means a larger talent pool

The flood of applicants is real: 99.9% use the "spray and pray" approach. Effective filtering requires rigorous, unique screening questions that demand actual effort. Avoid agencies, as they sometimes bait-and-switch with senior engineers for the screening then shift to juniors. Watch for candidates who want the job solely because it is remote, not because they believe in the product.

## Remote is easier for engineering

Engineers tend to be more self-motivated and require less handholding than other roles (like sales). Seniority matters too: senior people work better remotely regardless of position.

## Habits will make or break you

The most important habit: mentally trigger a beginning and end to your work day. Don't sit in PJs all day. Have a routine. Petersson's schedule includes a fixed wake time, reading, exercise, defined work hours (09:30 to 19:30), and a hard stop with no office access after hours.

## Sleep and switching off

The startup hustle-porn of pulling all-nighters is a dying breed. Even if you clock more hours working late, you get less done. Switching off is critical, and it is much harder when working remotely. Keep a hard boundary on your work day.

## Kill the distractions

Move your phone and watch out of the office. Every buzz breaks focus, even if you don't check it. Check notifications periodically instead. For developers, minimal window managers like i3 help cut desktop noise.

## Don't skimp on equipment

Invest in a large 4K screen on a monitor arm, a standing desk, and a good ergonomic keyboard. Your body will thank you.
