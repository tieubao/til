## Context

HBR article addressing a common fear among managers: what to do when your direct reports know more than you do. The answer is a fundamental reframe of what management actually means.

**Source:** [How to Manage People Who Are Smarter than You](https://hbr.org/2015/08/how-to-manage-people-who-are-smarter-than-you)

## The mindset shift

Your role is no longer to be an individual contributor. Your job becomes enabling others' success, creating conditions where talented people can thrive.

## Core strategies

**Confront your insecurity:** Acknowledge fear as natural but examine whether it reflects reality. If no one has said anything to you directly or indirectly, ask yourself where the fear is coming from. Unaddressed anxiety signals weakness to your team.

**Seek external perspective:** Talk with peers, mentors, and your own manager about similar challenges. Ask leadership what strengths they see in you for the role.

**Learn from your team:** Shadow team members, ask substantive questions, demonstrate genuine curiosity. Get educated enough about what they do to have informed conversations.

**Provide direction, not instructions:** Set vision and success criteria while trusting their expertise on execution. Articulate what success looks like; let them determine how it gets done.

**Add demonstrable value:** Build credibility through networking, strategic perspective, stakeholder communication, and supporting career development, not technical prowess.

**Create space, not oversight:** Step back and enable performance. Maintain transparency about why you need information, which prevents distrust while allowing autonomy.

**Project calibrated confidence:** Balance authentic humility with executive presence. Neither arrogant nor fearful, but calm, respectful, and decisive.
